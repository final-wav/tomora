# Track 09: "Wavelengths"
## The Dissolution of the Solid Ego: Dissociative Oceanics, the Prison of Neural Frequencies, and the Quest for the Primordial Ground

---

### I. Album Dramaturgy: Context & Threshold Function

After Track 08 (*"I Drink The Light"*) saw the desperate attempt to incinerate internal petrification through gigantomaniac sensory flooding (*"the whole sun in my mouth"*) shatter into a mechanical glitch and a stammering linguistic collapse (*"-ted to feel"*), *"Wavelengths"* executes a fundamental metamorphosis of the album's entire sonic and psychic texture. The nervous system can no longer physically sustain this sympathetic overheating. The noise cuts out; the lashing big-beat percussion gives way to a cavernous, fluid, suspended acoustic architecture.

Yet this calming is by no means a return to a healthy reality testing or somatic grounding. Rather, it marks the entry into an **oceanic dissociation**. The ego turns radically away from the external world (from other people, from airplanes, from blinding light) and gazes inward into its own overstimulated psychic interior. It realizes that it is imprisoned not by external reality, but by its own neural frequencies—trapped upon the wavelengths of its own mind (*"wavelengths of my mind"*). Where preceding tracks fought desperately for action, epistemic certainty, feeling, or sovereign control, Track 09 lays bare the foundational ontological rupture of the entire album: **The total collapse of ego identity and the naked, unadorned question: *"Who am I?"***

### III. The Exhaustive Reading: 32-Point Deep Dive

#### 1. The Emergence of the Past Tense: "Once I was a river"
The song opens with the folkloric, elegiac signal word *"Once"* ("Once upon a time"). Following the crushing immediacy and frantic present-tense verbs of the first eight tracks, a vast temporal depth dimension emerges for the very first time. The ego recalls a mythical pre-state: it was once not a rigid, bounded subject, but a fluid river (*"a river"*)—at one with motion, devoid of blockages, unburdened by psychic armor.

#### 2. The Paradox of Childlike Wisdom: "Wisdom of a child"
The wisdom of the child (*"wisdom of a child"*) represents an epistemic clarity antecedent to psychic division and traumatic individuation. It is a wisdom that does not yet need to demarcate ego from world, that harbors no impulse to control (*A Boy Like You*), sound alarms (*Ring The Alarm*), or voraciously ingest (*I Drink The Light*). The subject mourns the catastrophic forfeiture of this uncorrupted, primordial state.

#### 3. The Impossible Metaphor: "Avalanche of raindrops"
*"Like an avalanche of raindrops"*—an avalanche is composed of compacted, frozen snow; raindrops are liquid, discreet, and dispersed. An "avalanche of raindrops" paradoxically fuses the crushing, catastrophic kinetic force of a natural disaster with the pliant yielding of water. It delineates an affective state that is simultaneously liquid and crushingly massive.

#### 4. The Trajectory of Sorrow: "From the mountain to your eye"
The vectorial trajectory descends from sublime natural elevation (*mountain*) directly into the sensory receptor of the other: *"to your eye"*. The drop lands inside the counterpart's eye—either as a tear or as an optical obscuration. Here, for a fleeting line, relational attachment flickers once more: that which originated as an untamed force of nature condenses in the gaze of the other into grief.

#### 5. The Loss of Space and Orientation: "No forward, no behind"
*"And I don't know how I got here, no forward, no behind"*. The subject registers a state of absolute spatiotemporal disorientation. There is no longer any forward trajectory (no future horizon, no illusory progress as in Track 02 *Come Closer*) and no backward retreat. The vectors of volition and intent have entirely canceled each other out. The subject finds itself in a state of absolute kinetic arrest.

#### 6. The Prison of Oscillation: "Still standing on the wavelengths of my mind"
And here the text articulates its foundational leitmotif: *"Just still standing on the wavelengths of my mind"*.
The verb *"standing"* represents a physical and hydrodynamic paradox when applied to waves: one cannot stand on a wave, unless it constitutes a **standing wave**—a resonant, stationary interference pattern that ceases to propagate through space. The ego is frozen solid upon its own neural frequencies. The psyche has recognized that it does not touch the world at all; rather, it remains trapped inside its own self-generated mental projections.

#### 7. The Irreversible Fall in Verse 2: "The only way is down"
*"So far I've fallen, and the only way is down"*. After the manic hubris of high-altitude flight in Track 08 (*"chasing the speed of an airplane / height of a flood wave"*), absolute free fall ensues. Yet this descent is no longer met with panic; it is accepted with an eerie, almost resignative calm. Terminal velocity has not yet hit the ground, but the directional vector is irrevocably fixed.

#### 8. The Eruption of the Maternal Archetype: "The mother always saves me"
Abruptly, an entirely new psychic instance enters the album: *"But the mother always saves me before she lets me drown"*.
Who is "the mother"? It is neither a biological parent nor an empirical person. It is the Jungian **maternal archetype**, primordial oceanic safety, water itself, or Nature as ultimate container. Following the total wreckage of the romantic partnership, the ego regresses into primary infancy: it surrenders unconditionally to a higher, maternal authority.

#### 9. The Cruel Ambivalence of Rescue: Toying with Asphyxiation
Notice the dialectical cruelty embedded within the phrasing: *"saves me before she lets me drown"*.
The maternal force prevents ultimate asphyxiation, yet she permits the subject to sink to the very precipice of drowning. Rescue exists here solely as chronic re-traumatization: the ego is never hauled onto dry land, but perpetually suspended in water—held precisely at the liminal threshold between survival and death.

#### 10. Renouncing Worldly Validation: "The world might forget me"
*"And the world might forget me, but I won't regret"*. In prior tracks, the ego fought tooth and nail for recognition: Track 04 demanded that alarms wail; Track 06 cried out in desperation: *"Have you seen me dance?"*. Here, the subject lets this narcissistic demand collapse completely. The world is permitted to forget; the compulsive addiction to interpersonal validation implodes.

#### 11. Eradication of Traces: "Leaving no traces on the hearts that I've met"
This constitutes one of the most devastatingly melancholic lines in the entire work: *"Leaving no traces on the hearts that I've met"*.
In Track 07, the subject violently carved the other's name into its own flesh (*"I got your name carved on my body"*). Here, it desires the exact diametrical opposite: no inscription, no scar, no imprint. It yearns to pass through the lives of others utterly traceless—like water closing over a dropped pebble as though it had never broken the surface.

#### 12. Repetitive Meditation in the Pre-Chorus: "On the hearts that I've met"
The pre-chorus isolates this syntactic fragment and repeats it twice: *"On the hearts that I've met / On the hearts that I've met"*. This is not the hysterical hammering of earlier tracks, but a gentle, respiratory contemplation. The ego releases the long-held fantasy of surviving in the relational memory of others.

#### 13. The Invocation of the "White Sailor" in Verse 3: The Mythological Ferryman
In the third verse, the ego addresses an enigmatic, spectral figure: *"Oh, white sailor, is there time / To sail beyond the wavelengths of my mind?"*.
The *"white sailor"* assumes archetypal, mythic contours: reminiscent of Charon ferrying souls across the river of death, or an oneiric psychopomp. The color white signifies purity, but equally the spectral void, terminal dissociation, and the total absence of contour.

#### 14. The Yearning for Transcendence: Beyond Neural Frequencies
The inquiry posed to the sailor is radically existential: *"is there time to sail beyond the wavelengths of my mind?"*.
The subject no longer inquires about love, proximity, or affective sensation. It asks for **transcendence**. Is there a realm situated beyond one's own neurotic cognition? Is it possible to navigate beyond one's own neural firing patterns without succumbing to total ontological annihilation?

#### 15. Lingering in the Hum: "Mm, the wavelengths of my mind, mm"
No spoken response arrives. In place of an answer, the vocal line collapses into a delicate, melancholic hum: *"Mm... mm"*. The hum resonates on the very frequency it seeks to transcend. The prison is elastic and coextensive with consciousness; one can sing of it, but one cannot breach its perimeter.

#### 16. The Chorus as Ontological Collapse: The Primal Question "Who am I?"
And then the chorus erupts—four stark, unadorned monosyllables, repeated four times:
*"Who am I? Who am I? / Who am I? Who am I?"*.
This represents the most radical caesura of the album. Every defensive role hitherto adopted has been demolished:
* The pleading child (*Please*) no longer exists.
* The superior analytic observer (*A Boy Like You*) is completely invalidated.
* The acute victim sounding the siren (*Ring The Alarm*) has exhausted her cries.
* The symbiotic paramour (*My Baby*) has dissolved.
* The solitary dancer (*Have You Seen Me Dance Alone*) has lost her imagined audience.
When every defensive armor and fantasy of attachment has been shattered—**who or what actually remains?**

#### 17. The Refusal of an Answer
To the question *"Who am I?"*, no external voice replies. No affirming consolation surfaces (*"You are loved"*, *"You are strong"*). The chorus leaves the inquiry reverberating through a cavernous, empty hall of mirrors. The ego looks into its reflection and beholds no face. This is the unvarnished phenomenology of **ego death** (the disintegration of subjective identity).

#### 18. The Refrain as Counterpuntal Dialogue: "(Sail away) Who am I?"
In the refrain, a striking rhythmic counter-motif intervenes: backing vocals inject the imperative command *"(Sail away)"*, answered by the lead vocal's trembling question *"Who am I?"*.
A dialectical dispute unfolds between escape and ontological forfeiture:
* Desire urges: *Sail away! Dissolve into nothingness!*
* Terrified consciousness replies: *But who am I then, once I have sailed away?*

#### 19. The Preterite of Longing in the Bridge: "I always wanted to"
The bridge enters with a devastating retroactive confession: *"I always wanted to"*.
This impulse did not originate with the breakup or the traumatic rupture of Track 07: the subject has *always* wanted to sail away. The yearning for non-existence, for liberation from the excruciating burden of sovereign selfhood, was the hidden undertow of the subject's entire life from the very beginning.

#### 20. The Oceanic Mantra: Eightfold "Sail away, ooh"
Eight consecutive times, the bridge iterates the incantation: *"Sail away, ooh, sail away, ooh"*. The vocable *"Ooh"* elongates the syllables into infinity. The music morphs into softly cresting waves. It is a lullaby sung to a dying ego—a drifting outward into the boundless ocean of the unconscious.

#### 21. Tom Rowlands’ Acoustic Metamorphosis
Rowlands exhibits his consummate mastery as an electronic sound architect here: in lieu of abrasive transients and piercing saturation, he deploys warm, analog filter sweeps, suspended ambient pads, and a pulsating sub-bass heartbeat. The sonic space is no longer dry and claustrophobic as in Track 02; it possesses oceanic depth, resembling a descent into benthic ocean trenches.

#### 22. Aurora’s Vocal Architecture: From Torrent to Ether
Aurora delivers this performance almost entirely divorced from chest resonance. She leans into her signature silvery head voice, bathed in long, diffuse reverberation tails. Her voice no longer registers as a localized biological body within physical space, but as an acoustic wave breaking against an unseen shoreline.

#### 23. Somatics of Weightlessness
Physiologically, Track 09 witnesses the somatic transition from sympathetic hyperarousal/freezing to parasympathetic oceanic collapse: muscular tonus abruptly drops, respiration deepens and slows, and bodily boundaries begin to dissolve. This is the somatic reality of weightlessness—a total surrender that registers simultaneously as profound peace and as slow drowning.

#### 24. Repression versus Dissociation
In *"My Baby"*, the ego engaged in active repression and denial (*"I feel it"* weaponized against gnawing doubt). In *"Wavelengths"*, the psychic system dissociates entirely: it no longer wrestles against external reality; it decouples conscious awareness from the physical organism and drifts into the ether.

#### 25. The Deconstruction of Memory
The pledge of *"leaving no traces on the hearts that I've met"* constitutes the ultimate confluence of self-erasure and absolution: one who leaves no trace has never inflicted pain—yet neither has one ever truly existed. The ego elects total relational amnesia as the requisite price for peace.

#### 26. The Physics of "Wavelengths": Oscillation in Lieu of Substance
A wavelength represents pure energy in perpetual motion; it possesses neither solid mass nor static spatial coordinates. By reducing its entire existence to the *"wavelengths of my mind"*, the ego dematerializes itself. It is no longer an entity composed of flesh and blood, but a fleeting, oscillating frequency in the void.

#### 27. The Ambivalence of "Sail Away"
To "sail away" superficially evokes romantic tropes of freedom and open horizons. Yet within the context of this album, *"Sail away"* serves as a tragic euphemism for psychic withdrawal—a flight from any subsequent possibility of human contact. It is a voyage into an anechoic vacuum.

#### 28. The Absence of Resentment and Accusation
Strikingly, throughout the entire track, there is not a single accusation leveled against a counterpart. No anger, no bitterness, no impulse toward retribution remains. The trauma has cut so deeply that all aggressive cathexis has burned out. What persists is pure, crystalline mourning.

#### 29. The Dissolving Outro: The Triple "Sail away"
The composition concludes with a faint, threefold incantation: *"Sail away, sail away / Sail away"*. The music does not cut out abruptly as in *Ring The Alarm*, nor does it stutter in digital failure as in *I Drink The Light*. It evaporates over the horizon. The vessel slips beyond view; the ego dissolves into distance.

#### 30. Preparing the Threshold to Track 10 ("Side By Side")
Yet this state of disembodied ethereality cannot constitute the final destination on earth. One cannot drift eternally as an acoustic wave in the ether. Gravity and somatic embodiment inevitably reassert themselves. Having disintegrated into its constituent frequencies and relinquished all selfhood (*"Who am I?"*), the ego must return to solid ground in Track 10 (*"Side By Side"*): How does one continue to exist alongside another human being when one no longer possesses a defined contour?

#### 31. The Annihilation of the Concept of Home
For this fractured self, no home remains. Neither in the physical body, nor in the other, nor in society. The sole sanctuary left was the resonant chamber of its own neural oscillation—and that very space has now been rendered uninhabitable.

#### 32. Ontological Status: Purified Nothingness
At the close of *"Wavelengths"*, the ego has been dismantled down to its bedrock foundation. Identity has liquefied; sovereign control is dead; volition is extinguished. All that endures is an unanswerable, trembling question cast into the cosmos: *Who am I?* It marks the nadir of self-reckoning across *TOMORA*—the point where all rationalizations fall silent and psychic space is emptied for what must inevitably follow.

---

### IV. Synthesis: Dialectic of Form and Content & Dramaturgical Fracture Point

*"Wavelengths"* functions as the meditative, oceanic centerpiece of the album's second half. The formal architecture accomplishes a complete deceleration and dematerialization: following the manic friction of preceding tracks, the production glides into a vast, fluid resonance chamber wherein the ego observes its own history from immense psychic distance.

The unforgiving dramaturgical fracture point resides in the collision between the longing for dissolution (*"Sail away"*) and the existential panic of ego disintegration (*"Who am I?"*): the subject yearns to leave no traces behind and drift beyond the tortuous oscillations of its own intellect—yet the precise instant those ego boundaries collapse, the naked horror of non-being bursts forth. This suspended state of absolute alienation cannot serve as the final resolution. The flight into the ether compels the subject in Track 10 (*"Side By Side"*) to confront the most agonizing reality that remains: the proximity of two alienated bodies required to co-exist side by side, unable ever to truly touch again.

# Track 02: "Come Closer"
## The Centripetal Dictate: The Radicalization of Desire from Supplication to Bare Command, the Solipsistic Vortex, and the Collapse of Language into Parentheses

---

### I. Album Dramaturgy: Context & Threshold Function

Within the overarching macro-architecture of *TOMORA*, Track 02, "Come Closer," marks the first radical tipping point in the psychodynamics of the subject. While the opening track *"Please"* entered the scene in a state of extreme fragility and tentative surrender—an unchanneled, defenseless supplication diffusing into the vast expanse through the indeterminate comparative *"closer"*—"Come Closer" executes an uncompromising narrowing and calcification of the relational architecture.

The threshold crossing from Track 01 to Track 02 is tectonic in nature. A pleading prayer hardens into a bare imperative; open, ambient space contracts into a centripetal territory; tentative reaching solidifies into repetitive compulsion. Where *"Please"* held polite and protective particles as an elastic buffer between the ego and the world, thereby acknowledging the fundamental autonomy of the other, "Come Closer" eradicates every mediating instance. The subject can no longer endure the suspended unknowing of the first track. To evade impending fragmentation and the sheer panic of isolation, passive defenselessness flips into a dictatorial incantation.

Simultaneously, "Come Closer" establishes the inescapable structural foundation for Track 03, *"A Boy Like You"*. In "Come Closer," the ego demands physical incorporation (*"to me"*) with every fiber of its somatic and vocal existence. Yet because this centripetal vortex conceives the other not as a living subject, but merely as a filler object for its own structural vacuum, the forced approximation proves inherently toxic and menacing: the nearer the other draws in reality, the greater the threat of annihilation becomes. Track 02 unleashes that very gravitational pull from whose consequences the ego must frantically shield itself in the subsequent *"A Boy Like You"* through intellectual typologization and cold character armor. "Come Closer" is thus the energetic blast furnace of the album, in which desire strips away its mask of innocence to manifest as an imperial drive.

### III. The Exhaustive Reading: 24-Point Deep Dive

#### 1. The Erasure of the Protective Particle: The Traceless Disappearance of "Please"
The transition from Track 01 to Track 02 unfolds primarily through a radical linguistic ellipsis: the total extinction of the word *"Please"*. In the opening track, this formula of politeness served as an elastic shock absorber. It demarcated the fundamentally unbridgeable distance between two sovereign individuals and theoretically granted the counterpart (*Gegenüber*) the right of refusal. In "Come Closer," this buffer has completely evaporated. The erasure of *"Please"* signals the collapse of the social and ethical symbolic order: the ego no longer asks for permission; it no longer negotiates. The abandonment of this protective particle exposes a psychic emergency in which no further deferral can be tolerated.

#### 2. The Bare Imperative: "Come" as an Absolute Command of Motion
Without preamble, the track opens with the bare verbal stem: *"Come"*. Grammatically, this represents the most direct form of heteronomy—a bare imperative in the present tense. The verb tolerates neither hesitation nor liminal space. It demands an immediate kinetic reorientation of the other within physical space. Psychodynamically, the imperative operates as a gesture of omnipotence: the subject usurps directorial control over the motor apparatus of the counterpart. Motion is not permitted to arise from the other’s intrinsic motivation; it is imposed upon them by decree.

#### 3. The Insatiability of the Comparative: "Closer" Rather Than "Close"
Instead of the static adjective *"close"*, the text relentlessly deploys the comparative *"closer"*. This is the linguistic cipher of an insatiable yearning. An adjective would define an end-state where desire might settle into repose—a boundary at which motion halts. The comparative, however, categorically rejects any such terminus. It demands a permanent, dynamic undershooting of whatever distance currently remains. Even if the other touches the skin, *"closer"* demands penetration; even once penetrated, it demands total fusion. Proximity is staged as a mathematical asymptote: it can be intensified ad infinitum without ever attaining the requisite threshold of saturation.

#### 4. The Personal Vector: "To me" and the Erection of the Gravitational Center
In Track 01, motion still hovered in the open ether (eight iterations of *"closer"* without directional designation). In "Come Closer," this vector is ruthlessly arrested: *"to me"*. The preposition *"to"* establishes teleological directionality, while the pronoun *"me"* occupies the absolute terminus. The subject declares itself the sole gravitational center of the universe. Space is no longer an intermediary site of encounter between two subjects, but a funnel whose incline slants exclusively toward the center of the ego.

#### 5. The Desubjectification of the Ego: "Me" as Dative/Accusative Object Rather Than Acting Subject
The grammatical drop-off is striking: the ego does not enter the scene as an autonomous, active nominative subject (*"I"*), but as a passive accusative/dative object (*"me"*). The ego does not act; it remains in utter immobility, demanding that the world move toward it. Psychodynamically, this mirrors an infantile fixation: the subject experiences itself as a needy center that must be nourished, touched, and sustained by the environment without expending any kinetic energy of its own to bridge the gap. The omnipotence of the command masks a fundamental motor and emotional paralysis.

#### 6. The Total Erasure of the 'Thou': The Eloquent Silence of the Pronoun "You"
Although the imperative is addressed to a counterpart, the pronoun *"you"* does not appear a single time throughout the entire lyric corpus. The addressee possesses neither face nor contour, nor is he invoked as an autonomous subject. He exists exclusively as a functional void within the command. This depersonalization reduces the counterpart to a mere part-object in the Melanie Kleinian sense: the other is not a partner, but a phantasmatic prop whose sole raison d’être is to plug the narcissistic void of the ego.

#### 7. The First Syntactic Collapse: "To me, to me, to me" as Rhythmic Pulse
Already in the second line, the complete sentence structure collapses dramatically: *"To me, to me, to me"*. Verb and adverb drop away. Language amputates its own movement and retreats into the repetitive recitation of the destination. This is a primary-process regression: like an infant fixating in desperation on the naming of the object, the ego hammers its claim into space. The triple tricolon functions not dialogically, but autohypnotically—a rhythmic self-reassurance against impending void.

#### 8. The Monopoly of Repetition: Compulsive Action Against the Relational Vacuum
The unbroken repetition of *"Come closer to me"* (lines 3 and 4) obeys the structure of Freud’s repetition compulsion (*Wiederholungszwang*). Because the utterance yields no immediate gratification, the psyche cannot pause. Any unoccupied moment of silence would lay bare the cruel reality: that the other may remain distant. Permanent repetition functions as an acoustic barricade against the silence of space. Language transforms into a magical incantation intended to bend reality through sheer quantitative frequency.

#### 9. The Parenthesis as a Fissure in the Ego: "(Come, come, come)"
In line 5, the texture ruptures completely: *"(Come, come, come)"*. The typographic enclosure in parentheses marks the intrusion of a second psychic topography. Here, it is no longer the outwardly directed voice of the conscious ego speaking, but the uncensored drive-pressure of the id or a doubled superego. The verb now sheds even its directional orientation (*"closer to me"* drops away) and shrinks into a bare, staccato drive impulse: a threefold *"come"*. It is a panting of language that signals the forfeiture of all grammatical discipline.

#### 10. The Polyphony of Desire: Dissociation and Vocal Layering
Musically and production-wise, *"(Come, come, come)"* is layered beneath the lead vocal track as a whispering, intimate counter-voice. This generates an oppressive splitting: while the lead voice barks commands in the front of the stereo field, an anguished agency murmurs the identical order in a whisper within the psychic chamber drama. The subject is not unified; it is split into commander and supplicant. The typographic parenthesis in the text corresponds precisely to this psychoacoustic dissociation.

#### 11. Tom Rowlands’ Bass Architecture: The Physical Funnel
Tom Rowlands' production imbues the text with an unrelenting physical materiality. The analog synthesizer bass functions not as melodic accompaniment, but as a tectonic surge. Operating at a low, oscillating cutoff frequency, Rowlands generates acoustic negative pressure. Every strike of the sequencer drags the listener downward and inward. The bass sonically enacts precisely what the lyric dictates: it constructs a centripetal force field that the recipient's body cannot physically evade. The music acts as the executioner of the linguistic mandate.

#### 12. Sonic Micropolitics: Dry Center Miking and the Annihilation of Distance
Aurora Aksnes' vocal delivery in this track is exceedingly dry, devoid of diffuse reverberation, pinned dead-center on the spatial axis. This represents a masterly formal translation of the lyrical thesis: reverb would signify expansiveness, reflection, and therefore *distance*—the very condition the text seeks to annihilate. By projecting the voice dry and intimate directly onto the listener’s eardrum, the production simulates the accomplished erasure of space. The vocalist does not stand on a distant proscenium; she whispers and cries out directly inside the ear canal. Proximity is aesthetically executed by sonic force.

#### 13. Syncopated Escalation: The Threefold Insistence
Lines 6, 7, and 8 iterate the identical bare sentence three times in succession: *"Come closer to me / Come closer to me / Come closer to me"*. Across this repetition, the metric emphasis shifts. Through delicate syncopated accents pushing against Rowlands’ stoic 4/4 beat, a rhythmic friction ignites. The ego rushes ahead of the beat; it cannot endure the pacing of chronological time. This micro-rhythmic impatience betrays the hysterical core of desire: temporality itself is experienced as an adversary, because every passing second of waiting leaves the wound of separation raw and unsealed.

#### 14. The Second Return of Stammering: "To me, to me, to me"
In line 9, the reduction to the prepositional phrase returns: *"To me, to me, to me"*. This recurrence within the very same chorus block demonstrates that the syntactic collapse was no anomalous slip, but the foundational cyclic architecture of the track. The moment language attempts to stand erect as a complete sentence, it collapses under the pressure of affect back into infantile insistence upon its own territory.

#### 15. The Amputation of the Destination: "Come closer, come closer"
The chorus concludes not with the complete formula, but with another fateful truncation: *"Come closer, come closer"* (line 11). The directional target *"to me"* suddenly drops away. Why does the ego, at the zenith of the stanza, amputate the very point of reference it labored so hard to erect? Because at this juncture, desire tips into a purely kinetic delirium. It is no longer even about the ego as an individual; the sheer momentum of compression—the obliteration of interstitial space as an end in itself—takes command. Language forfeits its destination because the intoxication of motion suspends the subject itself.

#### 16. Semantic Implosion: [Non-Lyrical Vocals] as the Collapse of the Symbolic Order
Between stanzas, linguistic discourse breaks off entirely. What ensues is no longer words, but *[Non-Lyrical Vocals]*. In Aurora Aksnes’ oeuvre, such vocalises are never mere ornament. Psychoanalytically, they mark the instant where language as a symbolic order (in the Lacanian sense) capitulates before the violent weight of the Real. When the utterance *"Come closer to me"* fails to enforce proximity, language has no recourse but silence or a regression into preverbal phonation.

#### 17. Somatics and Phonation: The Guttural Primal Cry and Breath
In these non-lyrical interludes, Aksnes’ vocal technique pivots radically: from sharp, piercing head voice to throaty, guttural respiration and yodel-like glissandi. The diaphragm works audibly against the larynx. One hears saliva, the column of air, and the somatic pressure of the ribcage. The voice strips away its cultural veneer and becomes a somatic organ. Here, it is no longer an intellect desiring; here, an organism screams for physical containment and epidermal boundary.

#### 18. The Re-Entry of the Chorus: The Manic Attempt at Re-Stabilization
Following this eruption of the pre-linguistic, the chorus enters for a second time: *"Come closer to me..."*. This is no organic narrative continuation, but the ego's desperate attempt to reassert control over itself. Having plunged into unregulated vocalization, the psyche clutches frantically to its former scaffolding. The resumption of the familiar imperative functions like seizing a handrail in the aftermath of vertigo.

#### 19. Rhythmic Constriction in the Second Chorus
In the second chorus, the atmosphere has irrevocably compressed. Rowlands’ drums tighten, percussion layers accumulate, and the texture becomes claustrophobically dense. The subject no longer speaks outward into expansive space, but against a monolithic wall of sound. The repetitions of *"Come closer to me"* and *"To me, to me, to me"* feel increasingly hunted; the breath pauses between phrases dwindle. The noose tightens.

#### 20. The Renewed Silencing of the Destination: Priming for Exhaustion
As in the first chorus, the text breaks off at the end into *"Come closer, come closer"*. The omission of *"to me"* confirms the inevitable degradation of desire: it consumes its own substance. What originated as a specific longing for relational connection degenerates into blind mechanical drive.

#### 21. The Second Non-Lyrical Inferno: The Drive's Triumph over the Word
The second block of *[Non-Lyrical Vocals]* is longer, wilder, and sonically more distorted. Frequencies are driven through analog filters; the vocal line undergoes modular refraction. Here occurs the definitive defeat of discourse. The ego can no longer communicate; it can only resonate, vibrate, and drown within the mechanical labyrinth of Rowlands’ modular synthesizers. Drive has cannibalized language.

#### 22. Retreat into the Parenthesis: The Outro "(Come closer to me)"
Following the discharge of the second non-lyrical storm, language re-emerges in the outro one final time—yet under completely inverted auspices:
> (Come closer to me)  
> (Come closer to me)  

Both lines are enclosed entirely in parentheses. This constitutes the decisive formal event of the entire track. The phrase that was hurled into the world at the outset as an imperious, exposed command is now encapsulated, retracted, interiorized. The ego has realized that its outward roaring has fallen on deaf ears. The command has collapsed back into an internal monologue, a solitary mantra behind sealed lips.

#### 23. Fading into Reverberant Decay: Resignation in Lieu of Fulfillment
The repetition of the parenthetical line in the outro slowly decays within the production's fade-out. There is no triumphant final cadence, no redemptive union of two bodies. The song terminates in reverberant suspension. As the bracketed sentence dissolves, the truth becomes undeniable: nobody came. Distance was never bridged. The ego remains abandoned in the room alongside its dictate—exhausted, burned out, and scarred by the futility of its own compulsive architecture.

#### 24. The Aporia of Engulfment: Why This Track MUST Fail
Point 24 closes the analytical arc: the tragedy of "Come Closer" resides in its inherent structural impossibility. By demanding proximity not as an encounter, but as the centripetal subjugation of the other (*"Come"*, *"to me"*, erasure of *"you"*), the ego destroys the very prerequisite for genuine intimacy: the subjectivity of the counterpart. Were the other indeed to obey this command unconditionally, what arrived would not be a 'Thou', but an enslaved object or an empty projection. The ego’s hunger would remain unappeased. This fatal impasse propels the album with icy logic toward the subsequent track: if raw, unmediated craving for intimacy yields only isolation and exhaustion, the psyche must don the armor of typologizing detachment in the next step (*"A Boy Like You"*) to shield the heart from the next implosion.

### IV. Synthesis: Dialectic of Form and Content & Dramaturgical Fracture Point

The formal and psychodynamic architecture of "Come Closer" constitutes a masterly unity of dialectical contradiction and musical inexorability. The track is the clinical protocol of a manic escalation that shatters against its own hubris.

On the level of the **dialectic of form and content**, the piece demonstrates how reducing vocabulary to an absolute minimum does not yield clarity, but rather stages the acting-out of an obsessive-compulsive neurosis. The song’s linguistic material consists of precisely four lexical items (*Come, closer, to, me*). Through this radical economy, the subject deprives itself of any capacity for differentiation, reflection, or empathy. The form mirrors with clinical precision the psychic mechanism of fixation: consciousness is constricted to a single sensory-drive channel.

Concurrently, Tom Rowlands’ production works dialectically against any sentimental illusions of romance. The machinic precision of the beat, the centripetally draining sub-bass frequencies, and the clinically dry center-miking of Aurora’s voice deny the listener any comforting refuge. There is no warm pop sanctuary here. The music operates like a hydraulic press, forcing both listener and subject into the exact dead center of the sonic arena.

Herein lies the **dramaturgical fault line (*Sollbruchstelle*)** of the track within *TOMORA*:
"Come Closer" fails not by accident, but out of structural necessity. The attempt to overcome the existential loneliness of Track 01 (*"Please"*) through an imperialist dictate of proximity flips into its diametrical opposite:

1. **The Forfeiture of Language:** The successive amputation of syntax (from the declarative command to the stammering of *"To me, to me"*, through the parenthesis of *"(Come, come, come)"*, and culminating in the unarticulated *[Non-Lyrical Vocals]*) demonstrates that desire, at the moment of its radical liberation, renders the subject entirely speechless.
2. **The Solitude of the Sovereign:** Because the counterpart was linguistically obliterated (*no "you"*), the command reverberates across a deserted arena.
3. **The Retreat into Parentheses:** The outro *"(Come closer to me)"* signals the formal confession of defeat. The imperative is internalized; it becomes an open wound turned inward upon itself.

From this landscape of ruins left behind by insatiable desire, the psyche has only a single avenue of escape: **counter-resistance and emotional hardening**. If the unbridled demand for closeness results in total somatic and psychic exhaustion, the ego can never again allow itself to appear so naked and destitute. It must reassert absolute control.

Precisely at this fracture point, Track 03, **"A Boy Like You"**, anchors itself: there, the subject will freeze desire, power up biological machinery (*"Body, heart, rhythm, start"*), and dissect the other from a clinical distance (*"I know what to do with a boy like you"*). "Come Closer" is the indispensable, incandescent catastrophe from whose ashes the defensive armor of Track 03 is forged.

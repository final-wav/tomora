# Track 12: "In A Minute"
## The Return to Time: Conditional Hope, Self-Preservation, and the Sunrise at the End of the Tunnel

---

### I. Album Dramaturgy: Context & Threshold Function

Following the radical hollowed-out evacuation and the archaic skeletal oath of Track 11 (*"The Thing"*), where trauma was fixed as an implacable object and all fraudulent romantic illusions were interred, *"In A Minute"* brings the monumental work *TOMORA* to its conclusion.

If one reads the album as a twelve-part *Bildungsroman* of the human psyche, this final track represents the long-awaited, yet deliberately unkitsch catharsis:
* Track 01 (*Please*) began in absolute destitution, naked need, and symbiotic dependency upon the Other.
* Tracks 02 through 06 traversed the inferno of struggles over asymmetrical power, suffocating proximity, hypervigilant alarm, and the brittle illusion of monadic isolation.
* Tracks 07 through 11 enacted the agonizing plunge, the somatic grief, the manic glitch, the dissolutive unbounding, and the bone-bare farewell.

In *"In A Minute"*, the ego finally returns from the timeless petrification of traumatic fixation into flowing, historical duration. The title itself is a temporal marker: *"In a minute"*. Throughout this album, time has consistently functioned as a menace—the relentless, persecutory ticking in Track 10 (*"Time, time, time"*) or the catatonic arrest in Track 09 (*"no forward, no behind"*). Here, for the very first time, time becomes the **vehicle of hope**.

Yet the track steadfastly refuses a naive Hollywood-style happy ending: it does not proclaim *"I see the sun right now"*. It operates within the conditional / subjunctive mood: *"I would see, see the sun"*. It is a cautious, emotionally disciplined anticipation of luminescence—buttressed by the single most paramount ethical imperative of the entire opus: *"Don't you forget about yourself"*.

### III. The Exhaustive Reading: 24-Point Deep Dive

#### 1. Entry via Wordless Vocalises: The Voice Preceding Speech
Like Track 01, the track opens with pure sound: *"[Non-Lyrical Vocals]"*. Yet the somatic and acoustic quality of this sonority has undergone a fundamental metamorphosis across the preceding eleven tracks: it is no longer the anxious, infant stammer of *Please*, nor the hysterical *Aah* of *Ring The Alarm*, nor the dissociative glitch-stutter of *I Drink The Light*. It is a warm, resonant, oscillating choral timbre. The autonomic nervous system has settled; somatic resonance has been restored.

#### 2. The Imperative of Non-Oblivion: "Don't you forget about it"
The intro initiates with a sevenfold admonition: *"Don't you forget about it"*. Who is speaking to whom? It is the once-fragmented ego engaged in internal dialogue with itself—or the voice of a newly integrated internal agency (the matured Self) appealing to the conscience of the wounded child-part. The *"it"* indexes the entire traumatic topography of the album: the agony, the unassimilable object (*The Thing*), the harrowing lesson of catastrophic disillusionment. Do not forget it. Do not repress it anew. Do not relapse into obsolete symbiotic illusions.

#### 3. The Dialectical Turn in Line 8: "Don't you forget about yourself"
Following seven iterations of *"about it"*, the eighth line executes the decisive, salvific pivot:
*"Don't you forget about yourself"*.
This constitutes the ultimate psychological and ethical verdict of the entire *TOMORA* cycle. Across ten tracks, the ego had systematically abdicated itself in service of the Other: begging for proximity (*Please*), functioning as an empty projection screen (*My Baby*), annihilating its own boundaries (*Wavelengths*), and crumbling to dust at the feet of the counterpart (*Side By Side*). Here sounds the categorical imperative of subjective preservation: **Do not forget yourself.**

#### 4. The Rediscovery of the Self
*"Yourself"*—the reflexive personal pronoun. The subject finally acknowledges that it possesses an inherent dignity, its own psychical boundary, and an autonomous right to exist that functions entirely independent of external validation or the presence of a *Gegenüber* (counterpart).

#### 5. The Insertion of Temporal Latency: "In a minute"
The chorus opens with the titular phrase: *"In a minute"*. Chronometrically, a minute is brief (sixty seconds); in a state of psychological exhaustion and trauma, a minute can span an eternity. The idiom *"in a minute"* possesses a dual valence in English:
* Literally: In a very short span of time, momentarily.
* Colloquially: It will take a little while; have patience.
The ego no longer hurtles forward in manic acceleration as in Track 08 (*"chasing the speed of an airplane"*). It cultivates a healthy capacity for frustration tolerance: psychic redemption does not need to be violently extorted in this very microsecond; it will arrive shortly.

#### 6. The Grammar of Conditionality: "I would see"
Striking and masterful is the syntactic choice of the auxiliary: the text does not declare *"I will see the sun"* (future tense / deterministic certainty). It deliberately employs: *"I would see"* (conditional / potentiality). This shields the track from cheap sentimental kitsch. Luminescence is not posited as a guaranteed given; it is a genuine ontological possibility, an opening of the experiential horizon. The ego dares once again to believe in the light without succumbing to infantile fantasies of omnipotence.

#### 7. The Visual Stutter: "see, see the sun"
Note the subtle reduplication: not *"I would see the sun"*, but *"I would see, see the sun"*. The verb of visual perception (*see*) is palpated twice. Like a subject emerging from subterranean darkness after prolonged sensory deprivation, squinting to accommodate to glaring photic exposure, the ego blinks toward the dawn: *see, see...* The eye cautiously calibrates itself to the solar radiation.

#### 8. The Metamorphosis of the Solar Metaphor
This line must be juxtaposed directly with Track 08:
* In Track 08, the desire was manic, voracious, and annihilatory: *"I just want to feel the whole sun in my mouth"* (oral incorporation, autogenous combustion).
* In Track 12, the formulation is humble, differentiated, and sane: *"I would see, see the sun"* (optical perception preserved at a safe distance).
The subject no longer seeks to devour the sun; it merely aspires to **see** it. The healthy aesthetic and somatic distance between ego and world has been reinstated. One does not need to swallow the light to be warmed by its rays.

#### 9. The Twelvefold Litany of the Chorus: The Weaving of Hope
The chorus iterates this line twelve consecutive times. Where previous repetitions on the album signified obsessional compulsion (*Ring The Alarm*), hypnotic auto-suggestion (*My Baby*), or frantic despair (*Side By Side*), the repetition here functions as a **meditative weaving**. With every cycle, the fragile trust in illumination embeds itself deeper into neural pathways and synaptic architecture.

#### 10. The Interlude as Dialectical Anchoring: "Don't you forget about it"
In the midst of the euphoric crescendo, the composition suddenly arrests its momentum: two lines in the interlude summon back *"Don't you forget about it"*. It is a therapeutic reality-check: Do not relapse into mania! Do not dissociate into ungrounded euphoria! Keep the wound in conscious remembrance as you stride toward the sun. Genuine maturity does not erase trauma; it integrates it.

#### 11. Non-Lyrical Vocals as Solar Emergence
Following the interlude, the wordless vocalises surge forward once more—this time buoyed by radiant major triads. Tom Rowlands no longer forces the synthesizer filters into shrieking overdrive as in Track 08; instead, they open gradually like an oceanic sunrise expanding across a vast horizon.

#### 12. The Second Wave of the Chorus: Absolute Transcendence
The chorus returns for an additional eight iterations: *"In a minute, I would see, see the sun..."*. The arrangement swells into a vast, oceanic current of warmth. The rhythmic drive is relentless yet organic; no longer an abrasive lash, but the steady, dilated pulsation of a healed, regenerated cardiovascular core.

#### 13. The Bridge as Ultimate Radical Reduction: The Fourfold "The sun"
In the bridge, the lyric discards every syntactic auxiliary. There is no longer an *"In a minute"*, no tentative *"I would see"*. What remains is solely the pure, radiant substantive:
*"The sun / The sun / The sun / The sun"*.
Four bars. Four invocations of light. The shadow has receded; the celestial body stands at its zenith.

#### 14. The Purely Instrumental Finale: Yielding to Sound In Itself
Following the fourth *"The sun"*, Aurora's voice falls entirely silent. For its remaining minutes, the track surrenders completely to pure sound: *"[Instrumental]"*. This represents the highest form of dramaturgical respect toward the listener and the therapeutic journey: once everything has been articulated, endured, and integrated, linguistic signification has fulfilled its duty. The album does not sign off with a clever verbal epigram; it releases the subject into the vibrant, somatic immediacy of pure sonic presence.

#### 15. The Contrast with the Album's Inception (Track 01: "Please")
Juxtaposing the inception and conclusion of the album renders its colossal transformation palpable:
* *Track 01 (Please):* A fragile, helpless supplication for proximity (*"Please come closer"*), directed outward toward an elusive Other.
* *Track 12 (In A Minute):* A sovereign, inwardly focused ethical reminder (*"Don't you forget about yourself"*) and an unmediated orientation toward the universal sun (*"The sun"*).
The trajectory charts an arc from total heteronomous dependency to self-authoring subjective sovereignty.

#### 16. The Somatics of Regeneration
Physiologically, this track indexes the reactivation of the body's self-restorative mechanisms: chronic vasoconstriction releases, and the autonomic nervous system down-regulates into the ventral vagal complex (the social engagement and restoration system). The thoracic cage expands, and respiration becomes deep, unimpeded, and rhythmic. The organism can finally breathe without the specter of suffocation.

#### 17. Tom Rowlands' Production: The Alchemy of Light
Rowlands crowns the opus with production of peerless emotional gravity: a propulsive analog house/electronic groove that fuses bodily warmth with club culture transcendence, interwoven with shimmering synth pads that evoke molten solar rays. It is the triumph of sonic architecture over traumatic fixation.

#### 18. Aurora's Vocal Delivery: The Rebirth of the Voice
Aurora's vocals here register as released, adult, and profoundly grounded. She no longer needs to shriek in panic, stutter in dissociation, or whisper in fragile dread. She sings with full chest and head resonance—the voice of an integrated subject who has walked through the inferno and survived.

#### 19. The Deconstruction of Urgency
In contemporary culture, healing is often demanded as an immediate, instant commodification. *"In A Minute"* rehabilitates temporal latency: convalescence requires duration. One is permitted to sit within the tunnel knowing that while the light has not yet broken through, it will arrive in a minute. This composure is the true triumph over panic.

#### 20. The Self as an Inalienable Core
The line *"Don't you forget about yourself"* provides the definitive answer to the anguished crisis of Track 09 (*"Who am I?"*): You are not the sum of your relational wounds. You are not the manic illusion of dyadic enmeshment. You are yourself—and this irreducible self must be safeguarded at all costs.

#### 21. The Absence of Resentment
Throughout the entire finale, there is a total absence of bitterness toward the defunct relationship. There are no vindictive impulses, no retaliatory grievances. The past has been mourned and integrated; the subject faces forward.

#### 22. The Sun as Symbol of Autonomy
The sun shines upon all things—impartial, inexhaustible, and unbound. By orienting itself toward the sun in the final reckoning, the ego aligns with an energy source that is not contingent upon the mercurial caprices or emotional withdrawals of an external partner.

#### 23. The Circularity of the Album: A New Dawn
The track concludes in an extended instrumental that gradually fades like early dawn breaking over the sea. The album began in the nocturnal crisis of abandonment and concludes in the dawn of a new day. The circle closes not as a compulsive repetition as in Track 05 (*"round and round"*), but as a **spiral**: one returns to the baseline of existence, but elevated to a higher, wiser plane of consciousness.

#### 24. Ontological Status: The Resurrection of the Subject
At the conclusion of *TOMORA*, what emerges is neither a shattered victim nor an embittered cynic. What stands is a flesh-and-blood human being (*from the bone*) who has traversed the underworld of symbiotic entanglement, control illusions, and self-alienation—and who now looks into the nascent day with head held high.

---

### IV. Synthesis: Dialectic of Form and Content & Dramaturgical Fracture Point

*"In A Minute"* represents the majestic culmination of *TOMORA*. The formal architecture of the piece reflects the hard-won victory over traumatic fixation: it opens with an imperative self-remembrance (*"Don't you forget about yourself"*), expands into the rhythmic pulse of patient anticipation (*"In a minute, I would see, see the sun"*), and culminates in the radiant transcendence of pure luminescence (*"The sun"*) and the wordless instrumental outro.

In doing so, this final track fulfills the foundational ethical promise of the entire opus: it demonstrates that the collapse of a toxic, symbiotic bond does not constitute the extinction of being, but the indispensable prerequisite for the birth of authentic autonomy. The ego has learned never again to abandon itself. The trauma is not erased, but it has lost its paralyzing terror. When the final synthesizer chord dissipates into silence, what remains is not an empty vacuum, but the warm, indestructible pulse of life.
